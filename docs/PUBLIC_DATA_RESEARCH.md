# Public data research: LTA DataMall and SMRT

Research checked **17 September 2026**. This note records what can safely be integrated into Ngeebula, what requires credentials, and what is not public. It uses official LTA, SMRT and Google OR-Tools sources; it does not treat search results or inferred data as operational truth.

## Practical integration map

| Source | Access | Published scope and useful fields | Fit for Ngeebula | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LTA **Train Station Codes and Chinese Names** and **Train Lines Codes** static files | Public download; no API key shown | Station codes/names and line codes. Last updated Jan 2025 and Feb 2024 respectively; both are ad-hoc datasets. | Best official replacement for the unverified station-name/code/line reference, after a checked import and normalization step. | The portal description does not promise coordinates, operational ownership, depots, tracks, platforms, assets, or maintenance fields. Interchanges may have multiple codes and must remain multi-line selections. |
| LTA **Train Station** geospatial layer | Public static download in ESRI Shapefile format; no API key shown | Point representation of MRT stations; last updated Mar 2026, ad hoc. | Optional coordinates for station search/map display. Combine by station identity only after validating the downloaded columns and code coverage. | A point layer does not establish serving-line membership by itself. The portal calls it MRT, so do not assume full LRT coverage without inspecting the file. |
| LTA **Train Service Alerts** | DataMall `AccountKey` required | Ad-hoc operating-hours disruptions: `Status`, `Line`, `Direction`, `Stations`, free-bus/shuttle fields, and an LTA message with creation date. | Read-only operational context, if the product later gains a separately scoped incident view. | Active incident response is outside this prototype. It is not a maintenance-plan, possession, work-order, asset-condition, or crew feed. `Status=1` combines normal service and minor delays; it cannot prove an incident-free state. |
| LTA **GTFS Schedule (Train)** and GTFS Realtime rail feeds | DataMall `AccountKey` required; API returns a short-lived download link | Planned network; realtime service alerts; trip-update disruptions. Added in DataMall guide v6.9 (3 Aug 2026). | Potential future network/reference validation. | Transit-service data, not maintenance resources or safe-access authority. Returned links expire after 15 minutes. |
| LTA **Station Crowd Density Real Time** | DataMall `AccountKey` required | Query by `TrainLine`; 10-minute intervals with `Station`, `StartTime`, `EndTime`, and categorical `CrowdLevel` (`l`, `m`, `h`, `NA`). | Passenger-impact context only. | Categorical station crowding is not a crew workload, worksite access, possession, or safety signal. |
| LTA **Station Crowd Density Forecast** | DataMall `AccountKey` required | Daily forecast queried by `TrainLine`; 30-minute intervals with date, station, start and categorical crowd level. | Could inform a separately defined passenger-impact score after validation. | It does not predict maintenance demand, failures, repair duration, or safe work windows. |
| LTA **Passenger Volume by Train Stations** | DataMall `AccountKey` required | Monthly downloadable CSV: station code, weekday/weekend-holiday type, hour, tap-in and tap-out totals. Previous month generated by the 10th; requests cover up to the last three months; link expires after 15 minutes. | Historical passenger-impact analysis and pilot evaluation. | Aggregated hourly counts, not realtime crowding. Some interchange codes are merged, so joins need an explicit mapping. |
| LTA **Passenger Volume by Origin-Destination Train Stations** | DataMall `AccountKey` required | Monthly downloadable CSV with `YEAR_MONTH`, `DAY_TYPE`, `TIME_PER_HOUR`, `PT_TYPE`, origin/destination codes and `TOTAL_TRIPS`. | Network-level passenger-impact research. | Same recency, expiry and merged-interchange caveats; no maintenance or staffing semantics. |
| LTA **Facilities Maintenance v2** | DataMall `AccountKey` required | Ad-hoc lift maintenance at MRT stations: `Line`, `StationCode`, `StationName`, optional `LiftID`, and `LiftDesc`. | A useful read-only example of a public maintenance feed. | It covers lifts only. It is not a general rail-asset register, defect feed, planned work list, technician roster, qualification source, or completion record. Do not import it as Ngeebula jobs without operator review. |
| SMRT public website and publications | Public web pages/reports | Public descriptions of asset-management and data-driven maintenance programmes, including JARVIS lifecycle tracking. | Sector context and terminology. | In the official SMRT material reviewed, no public developer API or downloadable operational feed was documented for rail assets, work orders, maintenance windows, possessions, condition data, or crew/qualifications. Absence from this review is not proof that no private partner interface exists. |

DataMall states that dynamic datasets are live APIs available only with an Account Key issued to registered subscribers. Calls put the key in the `AccountKey` request header; most responses are limited to 500 records per call. Registration asks the applicant to accept the Singapore Open Data Licence and API terms. Static datasets are separately downloadable. LTA also lists on-request datasets that require a data-sharing form and NDA; that route is not a public, keyless integration.

## Recommended no-secret addition

Replace the runtime's **unverified station reference** with a versioned, repository-cached import derived from the two official static tables: **Train Station Codes and Chinese Names** plus **Train Lines Codes**. Keep the current reference until the downloaded files have been inspected and a reproducible transform proves that every curated shortcut and every supported station/line combination is covered.

The importer should retain at least the source station code, English station name, line code, source URL, source update label, retrieval date, and licence notice. It should split interchange codes into explicit station-to-line memberships and preserve one station identity with multiple serving lines. It must not infer depots, trains, tracks, assets, ownership, or work locations. The station point layer can later add coordinates, but only after its actual schema and coverage are verified.

This is meaningful without a secret because it improves the exact workflow rule Ngeebula already enforces: station-first selection and rejection of station/line contradictions. It also creates a reviewable refresh path. A live alert or crowd panel would add credentials and operational scope without improving the maintenance scheduler's core inputs.

### Download and coverage audit (17 September 2026)

The two static downloads were inspected rather than relying only on the portal descriptions:

- `Train Station Codes and Chinese Names.zip` contains one legacy Excel `.xls` sheet with the columns `stn_code`, `mrt_station_english`, `mrt_station_chinese`, `mrt_line_english`, and `mrt_line_chinese`. It contains 213 unique station-code rows. Each row assigns one code to one published line name; interchange membership is represented by separate station codes with the same English name.
- `Train Line Codes.xlsx` contains 12 rows with `MRT/LRT Line`, `MRT/LRT Description`, `MRT/LRT Direction`, and `Shuttle Direction`. Its codes are not a one-to-one key: `EWL` is published for both Changi Extension and East-West Line, while Punggol and Sengkang loop descriptions also repeat their respective codes. The import therefore preserves the catalog rows and does not use this file to invent station memberships.

The committed [official reference](../backend/lta_station_reference.json) contains those 213 source rows plus hashes, source URLs, update labels, retrieval date and licence provenance. It does not replace `stations_db.json` or change runtime behavior. The reproducible importer is [scripts/import_lta_stations.py](../scripts/import_lta_stations.py). Its network access is opt-in:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r scripts/requirements-reference.txt
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/import_lta_stations.py --download --retrieved-on 2026-09-17 --output backend/lta_station_reference.json
```

The spreadsheet readers are stated explicitly because pandas needs `xlrd` for LTA's legacy `.xls` station file and `openpyxl` for its `.xlsx` line file. Tests use the committed artifact and in-memory rows; they do not call the network.

Comparison with the current unverified `stations_db.json` prevents a silent replacement. The official file adds `DT4 Hume` and identifies `CE1` as Bayfront. The current file identifies `CE1` as Promenade and additionally contains codes `CC30`–`CC34`, `DT36`–`DT37`, and `TE30`–`TE31`, which are not covered by the older Jan 2025 official station table. Absence from that snapshot does not establish whether a station is operating in 2026. These differences require an explicit product decision. In particular, official `CE1 Bayfront` conflicts with the current `CE1 Promenade`; the importer records the official row and does not reconcile it by inference.

The offline reconciliation helper [backend/lta_reference.py](../backend/lta_reference.py) provides the integration contract without changing either JSON file. It checks both code/name and the explicit published-line-to-local-line mapping. It labels exact matches `matched`, corrects a conflicting name or line only in the returned catalog view as `corrected` while retaining `old_name` or `old_line`, appends missing official rows as `official`, and preserves local-only rows as `unverified`. It recomputes the interchange Boolean only from multiple codes sharing the same station name. The result includes display-ready publication, access-date and licence attribution. If the committed snapshot is missing, it returns the unchanged local rows as `unverified` with an unavailable-reference warning rather than failing startup.

The official static files are usable under the **Singapore Open Data Licence 1.0**, which permits commercial and non-commercial use, copying, distribution, modification, and adaptation. The app or documentation must show conspicuous source attribution and link to the licence, including the dataset access date. The licence excludes personal data, unlicensed third-party rights, patents, trademarks and design rights, and prohibits suggesting official status or agency endorsement.

## What public data cannot supply

**Implemented integration:** The runtime catalog now calls the offline reconciliation helper. The source JSON and saved work are unchanged. Its 222 rows comprise 211 exact matches, one corrected CE1 record, one appended DT4 record and nine unverified local-only records. The picker presents attribution and flags unsupported selected entries; legacy code/name conflicts are still subject to the API location checks. No DataMall AccountKey or dynamic feed is configured or needed for this change.

No reviewed public LTA or SMRT source supplies the fields necessary to replace Ngeebula's local operational inputs for:

- asset identity, configuration, condition, failure mode or remaining life;
- corrective work orders, approved preventive commitments, possessions or engineering access;
- repair duration estimates, mobilisation/hand-back time, tools or materials;
- named crew, shift calendars, qualifications, fatigue limits or authorisations;
- approval authority, completion evidence, or auditable override reasons.

Those inputs need an authorised enterprise source and a data-sharing agreement. Public passenger and service data must not be presented as evidence that a job is safe, a crew is available, or a maintenance window is approved.

## CP-SAT wording and measurement limits

Google defines constraint programming as finding feasible solutions among a large candidate set under stated constraints; employee scheduling is an example. CP-SAT combines constraint-programming and SAT methods and operates on integer variables. Its official statuses matter:

- `OPTIMAL`: an optimal feasible solution was found for the model and objective supplied;
- `FEASIBLE`: a feasible solution was found, but optimality is not established;
- `INFEASIBLE`: the modeled problem was proven infeasible;
- `MODEL_INVALID`: model validation failed;
- `UNKNOWN`: no solution or infeasibility proof was obtained before a stopping limit.

Accordingly, “optimal” means optimal only for Ngeebula's encoded constraints, input snapshot and objective. It does not mean the safest real-world plan, the lowest operating cost, the best passenger outcome, or globally optimal maintenance policy. A feasible proposal remains a proposal and does not approve execution.

Any return-on-investment claim needs a pilot baseline and a defined counterfactual. Useful measures include median planner time per accepted plan, requests resolved before deadline, avoidable conflict/shortage discoveries before approval, proposal acceptance and override rates, rescheduling effort, and data-quality exceptions. Cost savings require agreed labour, delay and disruption valuations. Passenger-volume or crowd data may support an impact proxy, but cannot by itself establish avoided disruption, safety improvement, reliability improvement, or causal ROI.
