## Implemented capture and edit choices

- The product sequence uses 1600 × 900 real Streamlit still screenshots from a disposable local demonstration database. Motion comes from crops, transitions, metric cards, and callouts; the edit does not simulate cursor clicks or a continuous submitted transaction.
- The intake sequence is an unsaved illustrative draft. The assessed requirements, proposal, approval, and execution views use prepared records; scenes 6 and 8 consistently identify the relevant record as job #2.
- The final edit contains no mobile shot, interchange screenshot, or live validation/error frame. Interchange behavior is conveyed by an accurate annotation over the single-line Clementi example.
- Names visible in the screenshots belong to the owner-confirmed dummy roster, so they do not require blurring. Credentials, local paths, raw provider exceptions, and secret values remain absent.
- The comparison retains **1/2 · 1/2 · 2/2**, the fixed inspection, and explicit synthetic limitations. It does not present the worksheet proxy as measured human performance or convert the result into ROI.
- The closing graphic describes optional Gemini assistance after catalog validation. It does not show or imply a captured successful Gemini call.
- News context uses original graphics with compact source credits; no publisher screenshots or copyrighted publisher media appear.
- Narration and scene timing are locked at 230 words over 114 seconds. The final edit uses **40 word-aligned subtitles** that preserve the narration’s punctuation; cues are ordered, non-overlapping, and bounded within the composition.

## Editorial direction

Use restrained cyan and amber callouts over the dark interface. Let the product screens carry the evidence. Use subtle musical beats for the eight-asset reveal, schedule generation, the **1/2 · 1/2 · 2/2** comparison, and the final approval message.

The news figures establish the scale and limited time available; they do not validate this prototype. The comparison demonstrates one constructed specialist conflict and nothing more. Keep the closing prototype boundary readable for at least four seconds.

## Final script review

The storyboard narration is verbatim with `NARRATION.json`. Its 230 words are paced across the locked 114-second edit; the shorter total is intentional because the generated voice clips already fit their scene windows.

The claims remain within the verified scope. The Singapore figures are attributed to their reporting sources and presented as one observed window and SMRT-provided scale figures. Product language describes modeled constraint checks and a reviewable proposal, without claiming measured savings, improved reliability or safety, guaranteed availability, predictive maintenance, live integration, or endorsement. The approval scene now makes clear that the interface decision is not operational authorization.

The final subtitle track contains 40 word-aligned, punctuated cues. Longer cues wrap to two balanced lines inside title-safe margins rather than shrinking below the project’s normal subtitle size. The cues are ordered, non-overlapping, and bounded within the 114-second composition; the short final cue has enough hold time to avoid a flash cut.
